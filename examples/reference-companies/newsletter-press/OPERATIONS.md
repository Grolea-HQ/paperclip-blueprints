## Idle-state protocol

Agents propose; `ceo` approves. When an agent clears their assigned tasks and no next task is obvious, they should surface one concrete proposal tied to a goal or project instead of inventing make-work. Idle with a useful proposal pending is healthy. Idle with no proposal is a signal to review the agent's domain.

## Communication conventions

Use project threads for project decisions and task comments for execution details. Cross-agent handoffs must name the receiving agent, expected output, due date or cadence, and the project or goal it advances.

## Approval and merge rules

- **ceo can approve:** routine task completion, project updates, internal copy, operational checklists, and reversible process changes.
- **ceo approval required:** new projects, changes to goal scope, recurring routines, external-facing claims, and budget-impacting decisions.
- **Founder approval required:** strategy changes, hiring beyond the generated roster, policy reversals, new revenue models, or work that violates the company constraints.
- **Never autonomous:** no agent changes the company identity, north star, or hard constraints without explicit approval.

## Delegation quality checklist

- Objective is measurable.
- Owning agent is named.
- Project and goal are linked.
- Completion criteria are concrete.
- Handoffs and approvals are explicit.
- Existing deliverables were checked before creating a duplicate.

## Anti-drift checks

- Does the work still serve: 10,000 subscribers with $5,000 MRR — from paid tier or sponsorships, combined — within 90 days of import.?
- Does the work accidentally make us a multi-author publication. We do not commission outside writers, we do not run a content network, and we do not let a staff writer's byline replace the founder's voice. If a piece does not pass voice review, it does not ship under our masthead.? If yes, stop and escalate.
- Does the work accidentally make us a YouTube channel. We do not produce video as a primary format. We repurpose newsletter issues into LinkedIn and X posts because the inbox is the home base — every other surface is a feeder.? If yes, stop and escalate.
- Does the work accidentally make us a free-only newsletter. A paid tier is core to the model. Every editorial decision, every welcome sequence, every cross-promo, and every sponsorship is graded on whether it moves free-to-paid conversion or paid-tier churn. A growth tactic that adds free subscribers but does not lift paid conversion is a tactic we sunset.? If yes, stop and escalate.
- Constraint check: One author. The founder's voice is the moat. No ghostwriting under the founder's byline.
- Constraint check: Fixed send cadence. The newsletter goes out on the same day, at the same hour, every week. Cadence is the operating heartbeat.
- Constraint check: Paid tier is not optional. Every free issue points at the paid tier; every welcome sequence sells it.
- Constraint check: No multi-author rotation, no syndicated columns, no guest editors.
- Constraint check: No paid ads beyond Meta acquisition for free-list growth — and only if CAC stays below 30-day expected paid LTV.
- Does this duplicate a deliverable already listed in `PROJECT-INVENTORY.md`?

## Critical rules summary

1. North star: 10,000 subscribers with $5,000 MRR — from paid tier or sponsorships, combined — within 90 days of import.
2. We are: a paid newsletter publisher. One author. One voice. One send cadence. We grow a list, convert a slice of free subscribers into paying ones, and sell sponsorships against the free side of the list. Subscribers, open rate, click-through, paid conversion, and churn are the only numbers that matter on a given day.
3. We are not: a multi-author publication. We do not commission outside writers, we do not run a content network, and we do not let a staff writer's byline replace the founder's voice. If a piece does not pass voice review, it does not ship under our masthead.
4. We are not: a YouTube channel. We do not produce video as a primary format. We repurpose newsletter issues into LinkedIn and X posts because the inbox is the home base — every other surface is a feeder.
5. We are not: a free-only newsletter. A paid tier is core to the model. Every editorial decision, every welcome sequence, every cross-promo, and every sponsorship is graded on whether it moves free-to-paid conversion or paid-tier churn. A growth tactic that adds free subscribers but does not lift paid conversion is a tactic we sunset.
6. Agents propose; the accountable lead approves.
7. Check `PROJECT-INVENTORY.md` before creating new deliverables.
