# cross-promo-outreach

*How Newsletter Press swaps recommendations with other newsletters on SparkLoop and beehiiv Boosts — without buying dead lists or letting an off-voice partner ride under our masthead.*

## When to load this skill

- The Cross-Promo Lead is opening the weekly swap-pipeline review (every Wednesday).
- A new SparkLoop suggestion arrives in the inbox and needs vetting before reply.
- The Growth Lead reports free-tier signups are flat for two consecutive weeks and the cross-promo channel is being audited.
- The Sponsorship Sales Lead flags a sponsor whose newsletter would be a better swap partner than an advertiser.
- The Paid Acquisition Lead is comparing CAC across channels and needs the cross-promo cost-per-sub baseline.

## Inputs

- Our last 30-day open rate and click-through rate, pulled from the Open/Click Analyst's weekly report.
- Our active subscriber count (90-day-engaged, not total).
- A one-paragraph audience description in the founder's voice, kept current in `growth/audience-description.md`.
- The current SparkLoop or beehiiv-Boost partner list and the running log at `growth/cross-promo-log.md`.
- The banned-partner list — newsletters that previously sent low-quality subs, asked for paid integration, or shipped off-brand swaps under our masthead.

## Procedure

The pipeline has six stages. Every swap moves through all six. None get skipped.

1. **Sourced.** Identify candidates through SparkLoop matches, beehiiv Boosts, manual search in the niche, or direct referral from another operator. Log the source — referrals convert at 3x the rate of cold matches.
2. **Vetted.** Apply the disqualification gate (next section). If any single disqualifier fires, the partner is rejected and logged so we do not re-source them.
3. **Pitched.** One-paragraph cold pitch, in the founder's voice, with our rolling 30-day open rate, click-through rate, active subscriber count, and audience description. Never inflate. Sponsors compare notes.
4. **Negotiated.** Swap size, placement (top-of-issue vs. footer), date, and exclusivity window agreed in writing — in the same thread, no verbal terms.
5. **Live.** Tracking link issued via SparkLoop or a manual UTM, swap scheduled into the editorial calendar, copy approved by the CEO under masthead-review rules.
6. **Reported.** Subs gained, click-through on the swap, and Day-30 retention of the cohort. Logged to `growth/cross-promo-log.md`. Partners below threshold are not re-engaged.

## Disqualification gate

- Open rate below 25% — their list is dead; we get noise, not subscribers.
- Audience overlap below 60% with our niche — wrong vertical, wrong reader.
- Asks for a paid integration disguised as a swap — that is sponsorship, not cross-promo, and routes to the Sponsorship Sales Lead.
- Off-voice publication — political alignment, hype-driven copy, or anything the masthead would not co-sign.
- Has previously sent us subs with sub-20% Day-30 retention.

## Outputs

- `growth/cross-promo-log.md` — every swap row: partner, date, placement, subs gained, CTR, Day-30 retention, decision to re-engage.
- A short CEO-approval brief per swap before it goes live: partner one-liner, why they fit, what we send, what we receive.
- A monthly summary in the Growth Lead's roll-up: total subs from cross-promo, cost per sub (time, not dollars), top three partners, sunset list.

## Anti-patterns

- Auto-accepting every SparkLoop suggestion. The match score is a hint, not a verdict.
- Skipping CEO approval because the swap "looks fine." Every recommendation under our masthead carries the founder's voice as guarantor — every swap gets the same approval gate as an issue.
- Trading with a higher-open-rate but off-niche newsletter for the volume. Off-niche subs churn at Day-30.
- Letting a swap run without UTM tracking. An unmeasured swap cannot earn a second invitation.
- Re-engaging a banned partner because they have grown. Their list quality is downstream of how they grew it.
- Using cross-promo as the primary growth channel. It is a feeder. Owned content is the moat.
